Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Dearborn Heights

Many early failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
