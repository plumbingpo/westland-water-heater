What makes a water heater installation different from a replacement? Usually, it is the new work. Whether it is a heater for an addition, a relocation out of living space, an upgrade to a bigger tank or tankless unit, or a fuel conversion, the job needs piping, venting, or wiring the house never had. In Westland, MI, that work also has to meet local code, which can mean permits, drain pans, expansion tanks, and seismic straps depending on the location. Westland Water Heater plans the whole job up front so the heater performs, the installation passes, and nothing gets improvised on the day.

Good installations start on paper. Tanks are sized by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at your water's temperature rise, so the heater keeps pace with your busiest hour. Location is the next question. Gas units need a way to vent outdoors and enough air for combustion. Heat pump models need room, a space that stays fairly warm, and somewhere to drain condensate. An electric tankless unit may need a heavy dedicated circuit and enough panel capacity to support it. Above finished space or in an attic, the heater needs a drain pan piped somewhere safe. We work through all of it with you in plain language, then give you a firm price.

On installation day, every step gets verified. New gas piping is run and pressure-tested, a sediment trap goes on, and the vent is routed exactly to spec. Electric and heat pump units get a circuit matched to their load. Water lines get accessible shutoffs and proper metal transitions, and the relief valve discharge runs full size to a safe point. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and we handle the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Installation?

A new water heater installation is really several trades in one job. Gas piping has to be sized for the heater's input and every other appliance on the line, then pressure-tested and leak-free. Venting has to follow the manufacturer's rules for diameter, length, pitch, and clearance to combustibles, or carbon monoxide can spill back into the house. Electric and heat pump units need a circuit, breaker, and wire gauge matched to their load, and whole-house electric tankless units can draw more current than many panels have to spare. On the water side, there is more: shutoffs, dielectric transitions, a correctly rated relief valve with a full-size discharge line, and code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps that vary by location.

With a professional install, every piece is done right once. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your demand, calculate the gas line, plan the vent route, confirm electrical capacity, and install the code items your location requires. We test for leaks, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and leave the heater at a safe temperature with the paperwork in order. That is the difference between a heater that simply works for years and one that fails inspection or backdrafts on the first cold morning. And if a simpler option would serve you just as well, we tell you that too, without pressure.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
