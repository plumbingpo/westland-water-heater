Most tankless problems announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. That is the pattern we see again and again in Westland, MI.

Our approach to tankless repair is simple: measure first, replace second. When we arrive at your Westland home, we record the active code and the unit's fault history, then work through the systems behind it. Flow sensors are checked with water running to confirm they report demand correctly. Flame rods and igniters are tested electrically. Gas pressure is read at the inlet with the burner at maximum, because a line that holds steady at idle can sag badly under full load. The vent and air intake are inspected end to end, and the heat exchanger is judged by the temperature rise it achieves at a known flow. Only then do we recommend a fix, and we can show you exactly why.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. A descaling flush clears most mineral buildup, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. Warning Signs to Watch For

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A fault code on the front panel and no hot water
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Outlet temperature that surges and sags while water is running
  • Repeated ignition attempts, then a shutdown
  • Temperature dropping sharply when a second shower or the dishwasher starts
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Tankless repair is a poor fit for DIY for two reasons: the diagnosis is tricky and the hazards are real. On the diagnostic side, an ignition code might mean a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and replacing parts until the code clears adds up fast. On the safety side, a gas unit burns fuel at a very high rate and vents exhaust that contains carbon monoxide, while an electric unit can pull more than 100 amps through multiple 240-volt breakers. Errors with either type can be serious.

A trained technician with the right instruments gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. We descale with a circulating pump and the right solution, then flush thoroughly so nothing corrosive is left in your plumbing. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, we will say so before you spend money on a repair.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • If scale comes back quickly, add a softener or a scale-prevention cartridge on the cold inlet
  • Leave the power on in cold weather, since freeze protection only runs when the unit has electricity
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year so the flow sensor gets full flow
  • Have the gas line evaluated whenever you add a range, dryer, or fireplace
  • Keep the vent termination and air intake clear of leaves, nests, snow, and lint
  • Flush scale out of the heat exchanger annually, and sooner in hard water
