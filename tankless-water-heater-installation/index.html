A tankless unit rethinks the whole idea of a water heater. There is no tank to keep hot, so there is no standby loss while you sleep or travel. When a faucet or shower opens, the unit senses flow and fires, heating water on demand through a heat exchanger. The result is a continuous supply as long as demand stays within the unit's capacity, a compact footprint on the wall, and a lifespan that can be nearly double that of a typical tank. Families across Westland, MI often choose tankless during a replacement or remodel for exactly those reasons.

Sizing is where tankless installs succeed or fail. We work from two numbers, flow rate and temperature rise: add up the fixtures you realistically run at the same time in gallons per minute, then factor in how cold the incoming water gets in winter, since a unit that delivers five gallons per minute at a mild rise may deliver far less when the groundwater is cold. Then comes the infrastructure. A whole-home gas unit can draw several times the gas of a standard tank, which often means a larger gas line or confirming the meter can keep up. Venting must match the unit, and electric tankless models can need a large share of your electrical service, sometimes enough to require a panel upgrade.

We install every tankless unit with its future maintenance in mind. That means isolation valves for descaling, a gas line with a sediment trap sized for the burner's full demand, venting that meets the manufacturer's rules for length and termination, and a condensate line with a neutralizer on condensing models. Where water hardness is high, we explain scale protection options before the unit goes on the wall. Optional recirculation can shorten the wait at distant fixtures. Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A tank that cannot keep up with your household's morning routine
  • Renovation work that makes gas line and vent changes easier to fit in
  • Paying to keep a full tank hot even when no one is home

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Installation?

Buying the right unit is the easy part. The other half is making sure your home can support it. Gas tankless burners can fire at close to 199,000 BTU, so gas line sizing, meter capacity, and pressure under full load all have to check out. Vent materials, lengths, and terminations are set by the manual and the code, and condensing units add a condensate drain that should be neutralized. Electric whole-home units can pull more current than an older panel can deliver. Get any of these wrong, and the unit underperforms or becomes unsafe.

A professional install protects both performance and safety. We start with a real demand assessment, confirm the gas and electrical supply, and design venting that meets the manufacturer's requirements. The install includes service valves, a sediment trap, a correctly routed relief valve line, and a neutralized condensate drain where needed, then a test with several fixtures running. Just as important, we tell you plainly if a tank, hybrid, or smaller point-of-use unit would serve you better. The right fit matters more to us than the type of unit.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • A setting near 120F limits scalding and reduces scale formation
  • Flush the unit with a descaling solution annually to keep scale off the heat exchanger
  • Make sure the outside vent and intake are never blocked
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • Consider a water softener or scale-prevention filter if your water is hard
  • Protect outdoor or garage units from freezing, and keep power on during cold snaps so freeze protection works
  • Check and rinse the small filter screen at the cold inlet periodically
