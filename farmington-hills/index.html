Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Farmington Hills

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Farmington Hills

Around Farmington Hills, water heater trouble tends to trace back to a handful of causes. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be a real factor: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping you may hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling is worth keeping up with where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A tank-style water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod has often been eaten away, leaving the steel open to rust. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well may see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless heater in hard water should get regular descaling. And an older install being replaced often needs code items that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or seismic straps where required. All of these is fixable, and most are much cheaper when caught early. If something seems off, a diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one.
