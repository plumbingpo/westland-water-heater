Keeping Your Dearborn Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

Hot Water Headaches Dearborn Homeowners Face

Long before a tank floods a Dearborn garage, it almost always shows warning signs. Pay attention to rumbling and popping while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or showers that run cold sooner every month. These are early signs of sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Ignored, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
