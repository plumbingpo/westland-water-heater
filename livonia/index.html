Keeping Your Livonia Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.
