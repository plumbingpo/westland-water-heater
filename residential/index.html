Upfront Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Westland Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing for every home, handling everything from a failed heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

A lot of water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Watch for discolored or rusty hot water, a rumbling tank, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a spent anode rod, or a worn dip tube. An early service call at the first sign usually costs far less than cleaning up after a failed tank.

Not every call is an emergency. Plenty of homeowners call us to size up an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats a rushed swap after a leak. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Preventive Care

Maintenance Plans for Westland Homes

Each maintenance visit from Westland Water Heater is more than a quick drain and refill. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, checks the burner, pilot, and venting on gas heaters, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and confirms a safe water temperature. Small problems get caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the heater stays running at full efficiency.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. An older tank needs a closer look than a brand-new one, a tankless unit on hard water calls for annual descaling to protect its heat exchanger working, and a large family works the tank far harder than a couple does. We build each visit around your heater, your water, and how your family uses it, so every tune-up helps keep the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule pays off. A clean tank heats water faster and more quietly, a healthy anode rod keeps rust from attacking the steel, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that annual service costs far less than replacing a water heater even one year early.

Proper Sizing

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the flow rate it can hold at your incoming water temperature. That's why we never just copy the gallon rating of the old unit. We look at how many people live in the home, the number of bathrooms, plus large draws such as a jetted tub or high-capacity washer.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely keeps up after two teenagers start showering back to back, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water as long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line sizing, electrical service, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Westland

Homes in Westland run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Westland Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, rumbling tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that drips or discharges is a warning that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can scald a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, expansion tanks where pressure is climbing, and leak repair on drain valves, fittings, and relief lines.
